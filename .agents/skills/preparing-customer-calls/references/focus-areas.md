# Focus areas

Each focus area maps to four kinds of signal:

- **Spend keys** in `org_universe.invoiced_mrr_m1_per_product` and `forecasted_mrr_per_product`.
- **Usage columns** in `org_universe` and `account_health_scores_v4` (`e_<product>` engagement scores).
- **Config or behavior tables** that show what the customer built.
- **Slack channels** to add to the internal search in [data-sources.md](data-sources.md), plus keywords for the shared channel and Gong.

A focus area that is not in the table still works.
Pick the nearest row, then search the shared channel and Gong summaries for the user's own words.
Also report adjacent products: a semantic layer focus needs the warehouse and SQL signals too.

## What the focus section should answer

Whatever the focus area, the section answers the same five questions:

1. **Where are they today?** Spend, usage, and what they built, with numbers.
2. **What do they need, in their words?** Quote the need from Gong summaries, account notes, or Slack, with a link. Look back further than 60 days if the need is older.
3. **What do they already do outside PostHog?** The tools and workflows that cover this need today.
4. **What limits a fit?** Compliance, data residency, missing features, open bugs, and slow answers from PostHog.
5. **What should the caller ask?** Four to six questions that the evidence leaves open. Prefer questions about their definitions, owners, and examples over product pitches.

## Semantic layer notes

- An empty catalog plus agent calls to `metric-list` means their agents look for governed metrics and find none. That is the strongest adoption signal.
- Metric definitions often already exist in prose: scout prompts, dashboard names, invoice reports, and call notes. List them as candidate metrics.
- Metrics that need data the customer cannot send to PostHog are out of scope. Name them so the caller can set the boundary early.
